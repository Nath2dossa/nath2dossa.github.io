Ocean dynamics, from the Gulf of Guinea to the Cape Basin

I am a postdoctoral associate at the Rosenstiel School of Marine, Atmospheric, and Earth Science (University of Miami). I study mesoscale and submesoscale dynamics in the Cape Basin using moorings and the new SWOT satellite, and I coordinate ocean literacy activities in Africa for the EU-funded OPERA project.

Before Miami, I worked on the Amazon Plume (AMAZOMIX), on salinity during the S-MODE experiment at Woods Hole, and on SWOT moorings at Scripps. My PhD at the Federal University of Pernambuco looked at near-surface circulation off Northeast Brazil and at coastal salinity gradients worldwide.

Projects

SWOT in the Cape Basin

At the University of Miami, with Lisa Beal, I use moored observations and Surface Water and Ocean Topography (SWOT) satellite data to separate mesoscale and submesoscale contributions to sea surface height in the Cape Basin, and to evaluate how well SWOT captures them. This work is part of the QUICCHE project.

OPERA: ocean literacy in Africa

Since 2025 I coordinate the development and dissemination of outreach and ocean literacy materials in Africa within the EU-funded OPERA project.

FOCUS: Florida Current and sea level

Fieldwork in the Florida Straits, deploying and recovering CPIES and moorings (2024, 2026).

My PhD

Near-surface ocean dynamics of Northeast Brazil and coastal sea surface salinity gradients in the world ocean.

PhD in Physical Oceanography, Federal University of Pernambuco, Recife, Brazil (2016–2021), funded by a CAPES scholarship. Part of the work was done during a 2020 internship at LEGOS (University of Toulouse III, France) on the global analysis of coastal sea surface salinity gradients.

Main results were published in Progress in Oceanography (2021) on the western boundary circulation of Northeast Brazil, and in Remote Sensing (2021) on coastal salinity gradients. See Papers.
